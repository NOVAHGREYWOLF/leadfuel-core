## What the idea is (source, unconfirmed)
ETG Open-Source AI Radar v1.4 (self-mail 2026-11-09) lists JEV-9B (autotrust), a typed-decision
model that returns yes/no probabilities, option distributions and 0-5 scores, and suggests it for
lead routing and urgency scoring. Top action: a proof of concept on redacted, human-labelled leads
with a threshold that sends uncertain cases to a person. Everything about JEV-9B below (Apache-2.0,

verify any of it. The AUROC is a publisher number on the publisher's data and says nothing about our
leads.

## The question the experiment answers
Given a lead (or inbound item) with PII redacted, does a small local model that returns option
probabilities beat the plain baseline at (a) choosing the route/class and (b) knowing when it does
not know, at a threshold that sends the uncertain ones to the owner?

## Design
1. **Task and label set.** Fix a closed list of options before looking at any output. Start with the
   decisions Gate 1 already records: approve / reject (ignore), plus a route (reply now, reply later,
   scope campaign, signal follow-up, not a lead / supplier question) and an urgency 0-5. The labels
   come from the owner's own past decisions (`inbound_triage_label`, `inbound_gate2_label`) and, for
   route, a hand-labelled set. No label is generated by a model.
2. **Sample.** 150-300 items, stratified by label, redacted (names, emails, phone, company to
   stable tokens such as PERSON_1; free text kept). Split once: a development part (about a third) for
   prompt and threshold choice, and a held-out part that is scored exactly once. Redaction runs
   locally; the redacted file lives under `F:\Claude Sessions\handoff\...` and the raw leads never
   leave the hub store. Client leads stay out unless the owner says otherwise (client work is private).
3. **Candidates, all local.**
   - A: JEV-9B via Ollama (only after the owner approves the pull).
   - B: an already installed general model with the same typed output asked for in JSON:
     qwen2.5:7b-instruct-q4_K_M, and qwen3:4b-instruct-2507-q8_0 as the small one. This is the fair
     control: if a general 7B with a JSON schema matches JEV-9B, the specialised model is not worth
     an 11 GB download.
   - Output contract for every candidate: one probability per option summing to 1, a 0-5 urgency,
     temperature 0, a fixed seed, and a parse failure counts as "uncertain", never as a guess.
4. **Baselines.** (i) majority class; (ii) the existing hub ranker (`inbound_triage._rank` with its
   learned rates), run offline on the same features; (iii) a keyword/rule router; (iv) a logistic
   regression on the same cheap features plus nomic-embed-text vectors (already local). A model that
   does not beat (iv) is dropped; (iv) is free and needs no GPU.
5. **Metrics** (held-out part, one run each, report n and a confidence interval, not just a point):
   accuracy and macro-F1 per label; calibration (expected calibration error, reliability bins); and
   the abstention curve: for thresholds t on the top probability, coverage (share decided
   automatically) versus accuracy on that share, and the owner's review load (items per hundred sent
   to a person). The threshold is chosen on the development part and frozen before the held-out run.
6. **Decision rule, written before the run.** KEEP for scope and the hub only if, at a threshold
   giving at least 90 percent accuracy on the automatically decided share and at least 40 percent
   coverage, the model beats the best baseline by a margin larger than its own interval, AND it
   shows no label where it is confidently wrong more than the baseline. Otherwise DROP, or keep only
   the confidence output as a ranking feature. These numbers are my proposal; the owner can change
   them before the run, not after.
7. **Wiring if kept.** Behind a flag, default off; local endpoint only; it may order and label Gate 1
   but never approve, reject, send or draft. An unparseable or low-confidence result routes to the
   person. Per the cloud-path rule, a down local model raises and the item stays in the queue; it does
   not fall back to a vendor. No new egress, so no Law 9 gate is added, but any change touching
   `inbound_triage.py` belongs to the hub's owning desk and needs its own task and PR.

## Cost and risk
- Time: labelling is the real cost (a few hours of the owner's or a desk's time on 200 items).
  Inference on 200 items with a 9B model is minutes to an hour on this GPU. It is a batch job; if it
  runs long it takes the estate GPU slot and `scripts/heavy_lock.py` first.
- Risks: label noise from the owner's past decisions (he approves for reasons not in the text); small
  n making any gap look real; leakage if the dev and held-out parts share a sender; redaction
  removing the very signal (company name) that decides the route.
