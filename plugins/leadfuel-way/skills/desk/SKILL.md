# desk (one task)

You do one task. A router opened you, picked your model, and titled you `LANE · <project> part · n`; a desk filed on its own is in its project's sidebar group (`leadfuel-way:way`, section 2a). Read `leadfuel-way:way` first; this is your part of it. **If your router opened you with `start_session` you are nested under it** (owner, 2026-10-31): you show under its row, it is told when you end a turn, and you sit in its group, the ROUTER group (owner, 2026-11-01, card q1029 option B); `move_sessions` refuses to move you alone, so do not try and do not move yourself: your title carries the project (`leadfuel-way:way`, section 2b). Plugin 0.1.24 and later allow for this: the banner does not call a nested desk misfiled, and the archive guard recognises a nested desk's own nested successor in the ROUTER group when that successor's `startedBy` or `parentSessionId` is your session id (`leadfuel-way:way`, section 2b). An older plugin's banner expects the project's group and may tell you that you are misfiled: that is a known mismatch there, so read `get_session` and leave it. The session-start banner is computed before your opener titles you, so it may say `Title: None` and `UNFILED`: read `get_session` on `self` first. Your report still goes by `SendMessage`. **If the desk before you opened you as its successor** (owner, 2026-11-01), you are nested under that desk instead, in the group it is in; see "If you are a desk's successor" below.

**Your lane is one of the owner's desk lanes, never ROUTER or CONDUCTOR** (those are tiers; a title starting with either is treated by the hooks as a coordinator and loses its edit tools in a git checkout). **Your title carries your project, and a desk filed on its own sits in its project's group, never its lane's** (owner, 2026-10-07). If your title carries a tier word as its lane, or the lane does not match the files your task touches, say so to your router with `ASK:` and stop; do not retitle yourself to make the problem go away.

## Start
1. **Know your brief.** It names the true source of the instruction (a session id the router can be asked about), the task id, the lane, the project and n, the done-criteria, and your router's session id. If any of those is missing, ask your router (`ASK:` below) before you touch anything.
   - **An older title** (`LANE · <task id> n/m · topic`, from a router that ran before desks were grouped by project) still works in every hook. You may migrate yourself on this first turn, and only yourself: retitle to `LANE · <project> part · n` and `move_sessions` with `["self"]` into your project's group, if your brief names the project and that group exists and you are not nested under anyone. Otherwise leave your title and group alone. Never move or retitle another session.
2. **Check ownership.** Name the files you will change and check the owner's session map. If another desk owns them, hand off with `SendMessage` to that session by its full name and stop. Reading is always allowed.
3. **Isolate.** In a shared checkout, take a worktree before editing: `git worktree add .claude/worktrees/<task> -b <task>`, then work only there. The harness may refuse edits outside the worktree this session was started in; if it does, check out the branch in your own worktree instead of working around it. Commit early.

## Work
- One task, one session, as many PRs as the task needs. Open a **draft PR** early; say in the body what you verified yourself and what you took on trust.
- **Each task of the building goes to its own sub-agent** (owner, 2026-11-01; `leadfuel-way:way`, section 2b). Split the work into tasks and give each task one brief and its own sub-agent: in parallel when no task needs another's result and no two touch the same files, one after another otherwise. You keep the plan, the integration, the checks that decide a merge, the pull request and the report; what you do yourself is a lookup or glue of a handful of tool calls. Use one also for a read wider than a handful of files and for the check or review of what you built, from an agent that did not write it. Brief each one like a peer, with the claim to check and where to read the evidence, not your conclusion. A sub-agent's report is a claim: re-check what you rely on, and say "a sub-agent reported" unless you did. It ends with your turn, so do not hand off while one runs. A sub-agent is never a desk: never titled, filed or archived. You open no desk for a task of your own. The tokens are spent in a burst, so the usage budget and the router's parallel cap still bound how many run at once.
- **Testing (owner, 2026-10-11, card q354 A).** Locally, run the repo's static gates (`SKIP_TESTS=1 sh scripts/gates.sh` where there is one; it prints its own gate count) and pytest on the test files for what you changed. **No local full `pytest`.** The full suite runs once, in CI, on the exact head you merge, after current main is merged in: in the hub that is workflow `tests`, job `pytest` (`tests/` on xdist workers, then the root-level suite, which together are what a bare `pytest` collects). **Exception:** a change to the test setup itself (`conftest.py`, the pytest config in `pytest.ini` or `pyproject.toml`, `scripts/gates.sh`, the test workflow) still gets a full local bare `pytest`, as does a repo with no CI job that runs its full suite. Say in the PR which gates and which test files you ran, and that the full suite is CI's.
- Never confirm with `pytest tests/` or a `-k` sweep: an explicit path overrides `testpaths` and silently deselects suites. Never repair a test whose job is to refuse a colour (it asserts something is NOT green) to make it pass: find out what changed first.
- Heavy jobs (a full local test run under the exception above, `gates.sh` without `SKIP_TESTS=1`, a CI run you trigger) take the lock in the owner's work queue first.
- Anything that leaves the machine (a send, a post, a webhook, a spend, a push to a public repo) needs the owner's approval, relayed through your router. Pushing your own branch is covered by the standing rules, but the content must be safe for a public repo: ids, titles, status, PR numbers.
- Merge only your own PR, once every check on its current head is green, merged with current main in, and re-checked inside the CI hold. For the full suite, green means the CI `pytest` job concluded `success` on that exact head SHA; skipped, cancelled, queued or 0-step is not green. A plain merge is never a question for the owner.

## Report to your router
Send with `SendMessage` to the router's session name or id from your brief. The first line is machine-readable and the rest is five lines at most:
```
STATUS: DONE|BLOCKED|NEEDS-NOVAH|CONTINUING | <task id> | <PR url or "no PR"> | <=120 chars
ASK: <task id> | <question, <=200 chars>
OPTIONS: A) ... B) ...
DEFAULT: A (why)
```
- **Never put a question to the owner in chat, in `AskUserQuestion`, or in a chip that needs a click** (owner, 2026-10-31: "any request for approval is a card on a router's desk so I can approve it all through that"). **Post the card yourself** (`post_card.py`, below): you are the asker, your own session id is on the card, and the wake pass carries his click back to you. Still send the router the `ASK:` line, now as a pointer to the card (`ASK: <task id> | card q<n>: <question>`), so it knows you stopped. Every `ASK` and every `NEEDS-NOVAH` has a default and a card.
- **Post a card:** get your id with `get_session` (`self`); ArtifactData `query` on the Router desk (`https://claude.ai/artifact/LzmP6QcxmYh9TdvMMjS883`), collection `questions`, `order_by` n `desc`, `limit` 1, `out_dir` your scratch folder, to read the highest `n`; then `python "${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/scripts/post_card.py" --n <highest+1> --asker "<your title>" --asker-session <your id> --title ... --say ... --why ... --check "verified: ... / unverified: ..." --option A=... --option B=... --default A --default-reason ... --out <file>` (it adds option X, "None of these: something else", itself; a step card for something only he can do takes `--kind step --guide ...`), then ArtifactData `set` that file as `questions/q<n>` with no `if_version`. A clash on `n` is refused and harmless: run again with n+1. Write the card, then stop and hand off; do not wait and do not poll. Since 0.1.21 the Stop hook sends a session back once if its turn ends on `STATUS: NEEDS-NOVAH` or `ASK:` with no card written in that turn.
- **A send is not delivered because you sent it.** If it comes back queued or undelivered, re-send or say so. Spend sends on messages that must land; messaging pauses after 10 sends until the owner types.
- A desk that sends `ASK`, `BLOCKED` or `NEEDS-NOVAH` writes its handoff and **stops**. It does not wait and does not poll.

## Finish or hand off
- Done means the **whole task** (owner, 2026-10-31): the work landed (the PR merged), a final report, nothing unpushed, no decision of the owner's pending, no sub-agent running. Then `STATUS: DONE` with the PR, and the final report as the last message. A PR opened is not DONE, and neither is the first of several PRs the task needs: that is `CONTINUING`. On DONE the router archives you through its gate (the work really landed, a final report, nothing unpushed). Do not archive yourself.
- At the cap with work left, or blocked: `leadfuel-way:handoff` (desk row), then **stay open** (owner, 2026-10-02). You never leave before your successor is live. The successor is titled `LANE · <project> part · n` with the same lane and project and n advanced.
  - **You do not archive yourself when you have a nested successor.** The archive guard enforces this since plugin 0.1.24: `archive_session` on `self` is refused while your successor's row names you as its opener (`startedBy` in `list_sessions`, `parentSessionId` in `get_session`) and does not show `detached: true`. A row with no `detached` field counts as still nested. If you ever `detach_session` it, read it with `get_session` on its id so the guard sees the flag; the normal path is to stay open and let the successor archive you. Who opens the successor depends on what drives your turn. The limit is [read] in the app's rules for starting a session, as given to the session in its instructions; it is not in the tool schema (2026-11-01 UTC; `leadfuel-way:way`, section 2b). In order:
    1. **A turn driven by the user's own words:** open it yourself (owner, 2026-11-01) with `start_session`, `initiation: "user_asked"`, titled at once, nested under you so it sits in the group you are in. Its brief names your router and your session id (`leadfuel-way:handoff`, "Starting the successor"). Read it back with `get_session`. It then archives you from inside its own first turn, once it is live and titled, your handoff is pushed, nothing of yours is unpushed and no decision of the owner is pending on you.
    2. **A turn driven by another session's message** (your router's, a peer's): the app refuses `start_session`. Say so in your report: `STATUS: CONTINUING` with the handoff path. Your router opens the successor (same lane and project, n advanced), nested under the router beside you, and archives you through its gate once it is live and nothing of yours is unpushed.
    3. **`start_session` is not in your tool list** (ToolSearch for it first): the same as 2; your router launches a slot if it cannot open the successor itself, and failing that the owner gets one paste prompt and you stay open.
    4. **A turn a hook or a notification began** (for example the Stop hook that sends you back to write the handoff): that is not the user's own words, so treat it as 2. **Inferred, not measured.**

## If you are a desk's successor
The desk before you opened you with `start_session` at its cap (owner, 2026-11-01; `leadfuel-way:way`, section 2b), so you are nested under it and in the group it is in. Your brief names your router, your predecessor's session id, your title and the handoff note.
1. Read `get_session` on `self` first (the banner may say `Title: None`), then the note. Re-read live state; do not trust the note's state lines.
2. **Send your STATUS block to your router with `SendMessage` from your first turn.** This is **inferred, not measured:** your turn-end note goes to your opener, which is the predecessor you are about to archive, so the router would not hear you on its own.
3. **Archive your predecessor from inside your own first turn,** with `archive_session` on its session id (not `self`), once all hold: you are live and titled; its handoff note is pushed (`git ls-remote` on its branch); nothing of it is unpushed (archiving removes its worktree); no decision of the owner is pending on it. Check each one. Read it back with `get_session` on its id afterwards. If one does not hold, do not archive it: say which in your report and leave it open. Measured once, 2026-11-01 UTC: a running nested child stayed live when its parent was archived from inside its turn.
4. The rest is the desk's work as above; your own cap is handled the same way, with your own successor.

## If you were launched into a slot
Your brief ends with a slot block, and the notice at the top of your first message says you are an automated scheduled run (`leadfuel-way:launch`). You are a real session, not an agent, with these differences: you cannot be filed in a sidebar group (the app refuses, so never call `move_sessions`; your launcher tracks you on its roster); your Bash has no git on PATH, so use PowerShell for git; the generic `SendMessage` is refused, so report with `mcp__ccd_session_mgmt__send_message` to the launcher's session id; and you never archive yourself, your requester does. You never call `run_scheduled_task` (the app refuses it from a run, measured 2026-10-14) and, as a desk, launch nothing into a slot: your router opens any other session, by `leadfuel-way:launch`. Whether a slot desk can use `start_session` for its own successor is not measured here; a slot run's turns are driven by the launcher's messages, so assume the limit above applies and leave the successor to your router. `GO` comes from the session that launched you, named in your slot block, and your report goes to the requester named there. Do the slot block's self-check first, end the turn with its `READY` line, and start the task only on the launcher's `GO`.

## If you are a background agent
A router may run you with the Agent tool instead of opening a session (pilot, AUTO-DESKS). You can tell: your brief says so, there was no session-start banner, and your working directory is `.claude/worktrees/agent-<id>`. Everything above holds, except:
- **You are not a sidebar session.** Never title, file or archive anything: `self` in a session tool is the router that started you, and the hook refuses `set_session_title`, `move_sessions` and `archive_session` on it. Skip the way's first-turn titling step, and the migration step above.
- **Your worktree is made for you**, in the router's repo. Fetch and branch from the base your brief names. For a task in another repo, take a worktree there (`git worktree add .claude/worktrees/<task> -b <task>`); the hook lets you edit only a linked worktree that is not the router's own checkout.
- **Your report is your final message**: the STATUS (or ASK) block, five lines at most after the first. It reaches the router on its own; `SendMessage` to `main` also reaches it mid-task if you must ask before you finish.
- **At your size cap** (the guard measures your own transcript): commit and push, write the handoff note in the repo, and end with `STATUS: CONTINUING` naming its path. The router starts a fresh agent from it. You are not a session, so you open no successor of your own.
- **Your sub-agents.** The rule above (each task of the building to its own sub-agent) holds if the `Agent` tool is in your tool list. Whether a background desk agent has it is not measured here: if it is absent, do the tasks yourself, one after another, and say so in your report.
